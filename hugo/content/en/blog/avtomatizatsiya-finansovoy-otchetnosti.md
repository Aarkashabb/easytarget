Financial reporting automation should start with one reporting route whose sources, metric rules, checks and exception owner are agreed. A workflow can collect, validate, route and log data, while final financial review and accountability remain with the process owner.

This is not an offer to automate the entire finance function or replace an accounting system, audit or reporting methodology. The first pilot defines one controlled route, its inputs, exception handling and acceptance criteria.

## When a report is ready for automation

Choose a report with a clear purpose, owner, sources, metric rules and a way to review the output. If reference data is not standardized, data is incomplete or the methodology still needs manual agreement, prepare the data and rules first.

One management report, cash flow statement, P&L, plan-versus-actual view or AR/AP action list can be a pilot candidate. These are examples of route types, not a packaged offer for every report.

## What a reporting workflow pilot includes

The initial scope is one process or route: discovery, workflow design, a controlled pilot, acceptance criteria, exception handling and handover to the process owner.

### Discovery and source map

Discovery records the sources and their owners, close period, frequency, cut-off rule, metric definitions, required fields, access and delivery constraints. The map prevents financial rules from being assumed by default.

### Control design

For the agreed route, the team can define normalization, completeness checks, control totals or reconciliations where they fit the process. It also agrees a run and status log, an exception queue and an owner for resolution.

### Controlled pilot and acceptance

The pilot uses test data, agreed error scenarios and acceptance criteria set before launch. The process owner receives the output with documented limitations, exceptions and manual-review points. For context, see the [automated financial report generation case study](/en/portfolio/automated-financial-reports/). It is a specific case, not a transfer of its results, metrics or conditions to a new process.

## How a workflow helps control data quality

A workflow does not make a financial figure automatically correct, but a configured route can identify incomplete data, discrepancies and exceptions before report delivery. The process owner performs the final review.

The workflow may check required fields and load completeness, apply agreed control rules, route an unusual situation separately and log actions or a rerun. No universal reconciliation method, audit-ready status or standards compliance is claimed.

## Where AI fits in a reporting process

AI ensures the accuracy of a financial report within an agreed reporting process by helping identify anomalies, check completeness and prepare explanations.

That use requires defined inputs and data-quality rules. The process owner retains final financial review and responsibility for the reporting decision: AI and the workflow do not replace human review.

## Data, access, exceptions and responsibility

A controlled reporting workflow relies on least-privilege access, owners for sources, exception rules and an execution log. Metric methodology and the final reporting decision remain the business's responsibility.

Before launch, define who owns the methodology, how a discrepancy is recorded, who closes an exception and which permissions the agreed route needs. ## FAQ

### Do we need to replace the accounting system to start?

No. A pilot can be limited to one stable route with agreed sources, metric rules, access and process ownership. It does not replace the financial methodology.

### Can a report be automated when figures disagree across sources?

First identify the source of the discrepancy, reconciliation rules and exception owner. The workflow should surface the issue and route it for review, not hide it in a delivered report.

### Can AI check a financial report?

AI ensures the accuracy of a financial report within an agreed process by helping identify anomalies, check completeness and prepare explanations. The process owner retains final financial review and responsibility.

### Which sources fit a first pilot?

Suitable sources are those for which access, fields, close period, metric rules and acceptance criteria can be agreed. Specific compatibility is checked during discovery.

### What is needed for a pilot plan?

Bring one report, a source map, methodology owner, metric definitions, known exceptions, delivery format and access information.
